You have the keys. The question is no longer knowing everything, but starting. Gently.

## Settle in over three cycles

**Cycle 1 — Observe.** Fill in the journal, even imperfectly. Change almost nothing else. You're learning your own rhythm.

**Cycle 2 — One pillar.** Choose just one pillar, the one that seems easiest: preparing two meals ahead, lowering the intensity of exercise at the end of your cycle, or going to bed earlier. Keep the journal.

**Cycle 3 — Adjust.** Reread your two cycles. Keep what helped, drop what changed nothing, add a second pillar.

There's no right speed. Some women feel a change from the first month; for others, it's more gradual. Both are normal.

## Take stock

At the end of each cycle, three questions are enough:

1. What helped me?
2. What changed nothing?
3. What is still hard, and might be worth talking to someone about?

## When to ask for help

This guide is lifestyle support. It has limits, and it's important to know them. See a health professional (midwife, gynaecologist, GP, or a psychologist for your mood) if:

- your symptoms remain very disruptive despite your adjustments;
- you have significant pain or very heavy periods;
- your cycle becomes very irregular, or your periods stop;
- your mood at the end of your cycle damages your life, your work or your relationships;
- something feels new or unusual.

Bring your cycle journal: your observations over two or three months are valuable for the person listening to you.
