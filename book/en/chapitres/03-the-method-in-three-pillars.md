The method comes down to three pillars. None of them is revolutionary on its own. It's their combination, and adapting them to your phase, that makes the difference. Start with the one that speaks to you most. You don't need to change everything this month.

## Pillar 1 — Nourish

In the luteal phase, many women are hungrier, have stronger cravings and digest less well. Rather than fighting it, we organise.

### Regular meals

Skipping a meal or pushing it back often sets up the 5 pm craving or evening snacking. At the end of your cycle, try to keep meals at roughly fixed times, and plan an afternoon snack if you feel the dip coming.

### A complete plate

At every meal, think of four food groups:

- **vegetables**, cooked if your digestion is sensitive;
- **a source of protein**: eggs, fish, meat, pulses, tofu, dairy;
- **mostly wholegrain starches**: oats, brown rice, sourdough bread, sweet potato, buckwheat, quinoa;
- **healthy fats**: olive or rapeseed oil, nuts, seeds, avocado, small oily fish.

A complete plate keeps you going longer and limits the energy rollercoaster. It's the basis of every recipe in this guide.

### Sugar cravings

They're common and have nothing to do with a lack of willpower. A few markers:

- plan **filling snacks** (a slice of bread, a yoghurt with fruit and nuts, a few dates with hazelnut butter);
- keep the square of chocolate you love, **without guilt**, ideally at the end of a meal;
- if the craving comes back every day at the same time, it's often a signal: the previous meal was too light or too long ago.

### Bloating and digestion

- drink regularly through the day, and choose **hot drinks** if they do you good;
- on sensitive days, **simpler** meals: cooked vegetables, soups, stews;
- eat more slowly, sitting down;
- notice what makes your bloating worse: your journal will tell you.

<p class="encadre-titre">What this pillar is not</p>
<p>It's not a diet, a slimming programme or a list of banned foods. There are no numbers to count. And this guide doesn't recommend any food supplements: if you're wondering about them, talk to a health professional, who will take your situation into account.</p>

## Pillar 2 — Move

Movement remains an ally at the end of your cycle. The question isn't "should I stop exercising?", but **"what intensity today?"**.

### Adjust the intensity, don't stop everything

At the start of the luteal phase, you can often keep your usual habits. At the end of your cycle, if your energy drops, lower the intensity rather than cutting it out: a shorter, gentler session, or a different kind of movement.

### What often feels good at the end of the cycle

- **walking**, ideally outdoors and in daylight;
- **yoga** and stretching, especially in the evening;
- **Pilates** and light strength work;
- **swimming** or aqua fitness, for the feeling of lightness;
- **dancing** in your living room, if it relaxes you.

### The right marker: how you feel an hour later

During the session, anything can seem possible. The real indicator is **an hour later**: do you feel more relaxed, more present? Or drained, irritable? Write it in your journal. In two or three cycles, you'll know exactly what to do, and when.

<aside class="encadre encadre-rose">
<p class="encadre-titre">If you train a lot</p>
<p>If you train a lot, the priority is to eat enough and recover well. Lasting tiredness, repeated injuries, or periods that become irregular or stop are signals to show a doctor.</p>
</aside>

## Pillar 3 — Read the signals

This is the simplest and most powerful pillar. It only takes two minutes a day.

### The cycle journal

Every evening, write a few words about your **energy**, your **mood**, your **sleep**, your **hunger**, what you feel in your **body**, and what did you good. The printable journal in this guide is made for that.

After two or three cycles, patterns appear: "my sleep gets worse around D22", "my sugar cravings arrive three days before my period", "swimming does me good at the end of my cycle".

### Your first signs

Everyone has their own early warning signals. Spot yours and write them at the top of your journal. As soon as they appear, you switch to "end of cycle" mode: a lighter diary, simple meals planned, an earlier bedtime.

### Organise rather than put up with it

If you roughly know the date of your next period, you can **schedule demanding appointments early in your cycle** when possible, and **protect the last week**. It's not always doable. But even one or two adjustments change the feel of a month.

### Tell the people around you

Let your partner, your loved ones, sometimes a trusted colleague know: "right now, I need more calm". It's not an excuse, it's information. It avoids a lot of misunderstandings.

<aside class="encadre encadre-alerte">
<p class="encadre-titre">When a signal is no longer "normal"</p>
<p>Pain that stops you from functioning, very heavy bleeding, new or worsening symptoms, or mood so low that it damages your life: these are not signals to handle alone. Talk to your midwife, your gynaecologist, your doctor or a psychologist.</p>
</aside>
