Here's how to apply the three pillars over the second half of the luteal phase. Days are given for a 28-day cycle: if yours is shorter or longer, go by **the expected date of your period** and count back.

This isn't a programme to follow to the letter. It's a framework. Take what fits into your life.

## D17 – D20 · There's still energy: prepare for what's next

Energy is often still there. It's the best time to prepare for the harder days.

**Eat.** Make an end-of-cycle shopping list (printable tool). Cook double: a soup, a stew, a cookie dough or a banana bread that keeps. Freeze portions.

**Move.** You can keep your usual sessions if they suit you.

**Slow down.** Spot the evenings that could be freed up the following week.

**Organise.** Fill in your weekly planner. Schedule demanding things now, and protect what you can at the end of your cycle.

## D21 – D23 · The slowing down begins

Sleep may get lighter, hunger stronger, patience a little shorter.

**Eat.** Regular meals, a complete plate. Plan a filling snack for the afternoon. Keep a hot drink you love within reach.

**Move.** Lower the intensity if you feel the need: walking, Pilates, yoga. Notice how you feel an hour later.

**Slow down.** Go to bed earlier. Cut down on screens in the evening.

**Organise.** Reread your first signs in your journal: are they there? If so, switch to end-of-cycle mode a little earlier.

<aside class="encadre encadre-rose">
<p class="encadre-titre">If you only do one thing</p>
<p>Go to bed thirty minutes earlier.</p>
</aside>

## D24 – D28 · Protection mode

This is often the most delicate time. The goal isn't to perform: it's to get through it as gently as possible.

**Eat.** Bring out the meals you prepared. Simple, warm, comforting. The square of chocolate or the dates with hazelnut butter, without guilt.

**Move.** Gentle movement: walking, stretching, evening yoga. If a more intense session does you good, keep it; otherwise, move it to the following week.

**Slow down.** Remove at least one commitment from your diary. Plan a moment just for you, even a short one. Warmth on the belly (a hot water bottle) soothes many women.

**Organise.** Put up your **D24–D28 emergency plan** (printable tool). Let the people around you know. When everything feels heavy, you no longer have to decide: you follow the list.

<aside class="encadre encadre-rose">
<p class="encadre-titre">If you only do one thing</p>
<p>Cancel or postpone just one thing, and tell someone you need calm.</p>
</aside>

## The first days of your period · The transition

For many women, the tension eases in the first days of their period. The body, however, may still ask for rest.

**Eat.** Warm, simple, nourishing meals.

**Move.** What does you good, and only that.

**Slow down.** Rest without guilt.

**Organise.** Take five minutes to reread your journal for the cycle that's ending: what helped you? What would you do differently? Write down just one thing to try next month.

<aside class="encadre encadre-alerte">
<p class="encadre-titre">Reminder</p>
<p>Very painful or very heavy periods are not "normal" and shouldn't be endured in silence. Talk to a doctor, a gynaecologist or a midwife.</p>
</aside>
