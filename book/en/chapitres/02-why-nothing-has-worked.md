You eat fairly well. You move. You've tried herbal teas, apps, your best friend's advice and Instagram's. And yet, every month, the same thing comes back.

It's not because you're doing it wrong. It's because most advice forgets one thing: **your body doesn't work the same way all year round, or even all month.** Here are the five most common traps. You'll probably recognise a few.

## 1. You do everything "right", but at the same pace all month

We're taught to be consistent. The same exercise routine, the same workload, the same sleep rhythm, from the 1st to the 31st. It's a linear logic, designed for a body that doesn't change.

Yours does. What works for you early in your cycle (an intense workout, a busy week, a late dinner with friends) can cost you much more at the end of it. The same effort doesn't have the same price depending on the week.

**What changes everything:** stop comparing yourself to yourself ten days ago. Your reference is how you feel today.

## 2. The effort trap

When energy drops, the reflex is to compensate. One more coffee. A harder workout "to get motivated again". An evening spent finishing what didn't get done. Less sleep to fit everything in.

In the moment, it holds. But at the end of the cycle, every compensation deepens the debt. And it's often just before your period that the bill arrives: exhaustion, irritability, tears.

**What changes everything:** in the luteal phase, slowing down isn't giving up. It's a strategy. You're not doing less out of laziness; you're doing things differently because your body is asking you to.

## 3. Contradictory advice

On one side, very medical information, precise but hard to apply on a Tuesday evening after work. On the other, vague, sometimes esoteric advice that promises a lot and explains nothing.

In between, you ended up trying a bit of everything, without knowing what really helped. The result: more tiredness, and the feeling that "nothing works for me".

**What changes everything:** simple, understandable markers, and one method to observe what works **for you**. That's what the cycle journal is for.

## 4. Restriction and guilt

Cravings for sugar or fat before your period are among the most common signals. The usual response is to "hold on": skip a snack, deprive yourself, then give in at night and feel guilty.

That cycle wears out both body and mood. Your body isn't asking you to punish yourself. Often, it's simply asking you **to eat more regularly and more completely**, to avoid the energy dips that trigger cravings.

**What changes everything:** replace "I must resist" with "I plan ahead". This guide offers no diet, no numbers, no bans.

## 5. Ignoring the first signals

The end-of-cycle crash rarely comes all at once. There are almost always signs a few days before: lighter sleep, a different hunger, patience wearing thin, a need to be alone.

When you don't see them, you carry on at the same pace until everything gives way. When you spot them, you can adjust a few days earlier, and everything becomes gentler.

**What changes everything:** learning to recognise **your** first signs. They're unique to each woman, and they repeat from one cycle to the next.

## What we do now

The rest of this guide rests on three simple pillars: **nourishing** your body in a regular, filling way, **moving** according to your real energy, and **reading your signals** to plan ahead rather than put up with it. Not more effort: effort at the right time.
