What's happening

In the days before her period, her hormones fall. Tiredness, sensitivity, a bloated belly, lighter sleep, a need for calm: it's common, it's physical, and it's neither a whim nor aimed at you. It usually passes a few days after her period starts.

  1. Know when. Ask her when the end of her cycle starts. Put it in your diary.

  2. Lighten the load. Take on one more task without being asked: meals, shopping, dishes, children.

  3. Listen. Without trying to fix. "I understand" is often enough.

  4. Offer. "Would you like me to…?" rather than deciding for her.

  5. Bring warmth. A hot drink, a hot water bottle, a blanket, a simple meal. Small gestures count.

  6. Give her space. If she needs calm, it's not rejection. Stay available.

Do

  • Ask: "What do you need?"
  • Plan quiet evenings and flexible plans
  • Believe her when she says she's in pain or exhausted
  • Simply say: "I'm here"
  • Keep your sense of humour… with her, not about her

Avoid

  • "Are you on your period or something?"
  • Minimising: "It's nothing", "You're overreacting"
  • Taking her irritability as a personal attack
  • Commenting on what she eats or on her body
  • Offering solutions she didn't ask for

When to encourage her to see someone

Intense pain, very heavy bleeding, fever with abdominal pain, or mood so low that she can no longer function: encourage her to see her midwife, her gynaecologist, her doctor or a psychologist. She is not unstable. She is cyclical. And now, you know.
