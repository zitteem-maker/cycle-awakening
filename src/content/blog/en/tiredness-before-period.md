**Feeling tired in the days before your period is common: it's one of the most frequent signs of the end of the cycle.** Hormones fall, sleep often gets lighter, and energy follows. It's not a lack of willpower. The right answer isn't to push through, but to adjust your pace for a few days.

## Why am I exhausted before my period?

During the [luteal phase](/en/blog/luteal-phase), progesterone dominates, then all hormones fall just before your period. Many women then describe:

- needing more sleep, or sleep that feels less restorative;
- energy that crashes in the middle of the afternoon;
- less desire to go out, see people or take things on;
- harder concentration.

If this sounds like you, you're not "less good" on those days. You're in a phase that needs more recovery.

## Is it normal to be tired at the same time every month?

Yes, when the tiredness comes back at the same point in the cycle and goes away a few days after your period starts. It's precisely this rhythm that lets you plan for it. On the other hand, tiredness that lasts all month is worth talking about with a professional (see the box below).

## What can I do about tiredness before my period?

Five simple steps. Pick one or two to start.

1. **Spot your days.** Write down your energy every evening for one cycle. You'll know when to slow down, and you'll stop being caught off guard.
2. **Eat regularly.** Skipping a meal makes energy crash. A complete plate (vegetables, protein, starches, healthy fats) and a planned afternoon snack often help avoid the slump.
3. **Move, but gently.** A walk, yoga, stretching: gentle movement often gives back more energy than it costs. Keep intense sessions for earlier in the cycle if they wear you out at the end.
4. **Protect your sleep.** Go to bed a little earlier, dim the lights and screens in the evening, keep regular hours.
5. **Lighten your schedule.** When you can, move the most demanding appointments before D24. It's not laziness: it's organisation.

> Slowing down is a strategy, not a failure.

## What if I can't slow down?

Work, children, life: you can't always ease off. In that case, aim for the useful minimum: a proper meal, ten minutes of walking, an earlier bedtime. And say it to the people around you. A simple "I'm at the end of my cycle, I need more calm" avoids a lot of misunderstandings.

## Tiredness often comes with other signs

At the end of the cycle, tiredness rarely comes alone. It often comes with [sugar cravings](/en/blog/sugar-cravings-before-period) or a [more fragile mood](/en/blog/crying-before-period). It's all connected: a tired body looks for quick energy and copes less well with frustration.
