**A swollen belly in the days before your period is one of the most common signs of the end of the cycle.** During the [luteal phase](/en/blog/luteal-phase), many women feel like they're retaining water, digest more slowly and feel tight in their clothes. It isn't weight gain, and it usually passes after your period starts. A few simple habits can make those days more comfortable.

## Why is my belly swollen before my period?

At the end of the cycle, two sensations often mix:

- **a feeling of water retention**: the body feels "fuller", fingers or ankles a little swollen;
- **slower digestion**: your gut changes pace, and wind is more noticeable.

Add to that the tiredness, which pushes you towards quicker meals, and autumn, the season of richer dishes: your belly feels it.

## Is it normal?

Yes, as long as it follows your cycle: it happens before your period, then disappears in the days after. If your belly is bloated all month, or if the discomfort comes with strong pain, it's no longer just your cycle (see the "When to see someone" box).

## What can I do about end-of-cycle bloating?

No diet, no miracle food: simple habits, to try one at a time.

1. **Eat at regular times.** Meals skipped and then made up for all at once overload digestion.
2. **Take your time at the table.** Chewing, putting your fork down, eating sitting down: it sounds trivial, it often changes a lot.
3. **Walk after your meal.** Ten to fifteen minutes is often enough to feel lighter.
4. **Notice what bloats you.** Some women find that very salty dishes, ultra-processed foods or fizzy drinks make it worse. Write down what you eat on the worst days: your own markers are worth more than a general list.
5. **Drink throughout the day.** Water, a warm infusion in the evening: a simple, comforting habit.
6. **Choose clothes that don't squeeze.** It doesn't reduce anything, but it changes the day.

> Your body doesn't gain weight in four days. It's going through a phase.

## And how I see my belly?

On those days, we often find ourselves "huge" in the mirror. Remind yourself that it's temporary, and that it says nothing about your worth. Avoid weighing yourself or judging your body at the end of your cycle: you'd see it more harshly than it is.

## What often comes with it

Bloating rarely comes alone: [tiredness](/en/blog/tiredness-before-period) and [sugar cravings](/en/blog/sugar-cravings-before-period) often come with it. Looking after your rhythm (meals, sleep, gentle movement) helps with all three at once.
