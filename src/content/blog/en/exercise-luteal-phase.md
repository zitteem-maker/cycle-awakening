**In the luteal phase, you don't need to stop exercising: it's mostly about adjusting the intensity.** Many women feel more tired, heavier or less motivated before their period. Walking, yoga, Pilates, gentle swimming or moderate strength work often suit well. The best marker is still your own: how you feel an hour after the session.

## Why do I have less energy for exercise before my period?

During the [luteal phase](/en/blog/luteal-phase), and especially in the last few days, many women describe lower energy, lighter sleep and sometimes a bloated belly (see [bloating before your period](/en/blog/bloating-before-period)). Sessions that felt easy earlier in the cycle can feel harder. Every woman is different: some barely notice anything, others a lot.

## Which exercise should I choose in the luteal phase?

Often, what feels good at the end of the cycle:

- **walking**, ideally outdoors: simple, accessible, effective for feeling better;
- **yoga**, gentle or more dynamic depending on your energy that day;
- **Pilates**, to build strength without wearing yourself out;
- **gentle swimming**, for the feeling of lightness;
- **moderate strength work**, with adjusted weights and volume.

You can keep more intense sessions at the start of the luteal phase (D17–D20) if they suit you, then ease off around D24–D28.

## How do I adjust the intensity, in practice?

1. **Drop down a notch, without stopping.** A run becomes a brisk walk, an intense class becomes a gentle one, three sets become two.
2. **Shorten rather than cancel.** Twenty minutes is better than zero, and it leaves energy for the rest of the day.
3. **Warm up for longer.** On tired days, the body takes longer to get going.
4. **Plan your key sessions earlier in the cycle.** If you have a sporting goal, put the most demanding sessions when your energy is high.
5. **Listen to the next day.** If a session wipes you out the following day, it was too much for that moment in your cycle.

> The right marker: how you feel an hour later. Better? You got it right.

## What if I don't feel like moving at all?

It happens. A ten-minute walk, a few stretches on the floor, slow breathing: even a tiny movement counts, especially on winter end-of-cycle days (see [end of cycle in winter](/en/blog/end-of-cycle-in-winter)). And sometimes, rest is the best session.

## What this article is not

It's not a training plan or a performance programme. If you train a lot and your periods become irregular or disappear, talk to a health professional: your body may need more energy than it's getting.
