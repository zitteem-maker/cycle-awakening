**When the end-of-year holidays fall during your luteal phase, everything is more intense: tiredness, emotions, digestion, patience.** It's not that you "ruin" the holidays: your body is going through a phase that needs more calm, at the time of year that offers the least. The answer isn't to cancel everything, but to plan ahead.

## Why are the holidays harder at the end of the cycle?

At the end of the cycle, many women are more tired, more sensitive and digest more slowly (see [the luteal phase](/en/blog/luteal-phase)). The holidays, meanwhile, pile up everything that tires you: late and heavy meals, shifted bedtimes, noise, family obligations, organising, presents. No wonder 24 December on D26 feels like a marathon.

## How do I know if the holidays fall at the end of my cycle?

Look at your journal or your app: find the date of your next period and count back. If the big moments fall in the days before it, you know you'll need to go easy on yourself. Just knowing changes how you live those days.

## Planning ahead: 5 steps that change everything

1. **Choose your moments.** You don't have to be everywhere. Keep the gatherings that really matter, decline or shorten the others.
2. **Prepare in advance.** Presents, shopping, menus: do everything you can in the first half of December, when your energy is higher.
3. **Protect your sleep.** Between two evenings out, plan a quiet evening and an early night.
4. **Keep meals simple on the other days.** Between festive meals, simple and light dishes ease digestion (see [bloating before your period](/en/blog/bloating-before-period)).
5. **Plan your way out.** A walk after the meal, a moment alone in a quiet room, leaving a little earlier: you're allowed to breathe during the party.

> You don't have to be everywhere. You're allowed to be there, at your own pace.

## And during the family meal?

- **Eat until you're full, without guilt.** The holidays are not a time for counting. Enjoy, and get back to your rhythm the next day.
- **Go easy on alcohol if it suits you less.** Many women notice that it weighs more on their sleep and mood at the end of the cycle. Alternating with water or an alcohol-free drink is an option.
- **Avoid conversations that lead to arguments.** It's not the time to settle the year's scores. Write them down, you can come back to them later if they matter.

## What if I break down emotionally?

It can happen, and it's not a big deal. Step away for a few minutes, breathe, drink a glass of water, and simply tell someone you trust that you're tired. If emotions become overwhelming at the end of every cycle, read [why we cry before our period](/en/blog/crying-before-period) too.

## How I do it

In December, I look at my cycle before filling my diary. On end-of-cycle days, I keep just one big moment, and I protect the evenings before and after. The holidays are no less beautiful: I just live them better.
