**Craving sugar in the days before your period is very common: it's one of the most frequently mentioned end-of-cycle cravings.** The tiredness of the [luteal phase](/en/blog/luteal-phase), an appetite that is often bigger and a more fragile mood all push you towards whatever comforts you quickly. It's not a lack of willpower, and the answer isn't to forbid it.

## Why do I crave sugar before my period?

At the end of the cycle, several things combine:

- **energy drops** (see [tiredness before your period](/en/blog/tiredness-before-period)), and the body looks for quick fuel;
- **appetite is often bigger**: many women are simply hungrier;
- **mood is more sensitive**, and sweet things comfort.

The result: at 5 pm, the chocolate bar is calling. It's common, and it's human.

## Should I resist sugar cravings?

Fighting a strong craving often ends in an even stronger one, with guilt on top. The idea is rather to **plan ahead**: respond to hunger before it becomes urgent, and enjoy it when you choose to treat yourself.

> This isn't a diet. It's a cycle.

## What should I eat when I crave sugar before my period?

A few simple markers, with no counting and no bans:

1. **Don't skip meals.** A complete lunch (vegetables, protein, starches, healthy fats) often limits the urge to snack in the afternoon.
2. **Plan a snack around 4–5 pm.** A piece of fruit and a handful of nuts, a yoghurt, a slice of wholegrain bread with almond butter, a square of dark chocolate with a few hazelnuts: something that keeps you going.
3. **Pair sweet things with something else.** A dessert at the end of a complete meal satisfies you more than something sweet on an empty stomach.
4. **Make treats that nourish.** A banana bread, oat cookies, stuffed dates: they calm the craving and keep you full.
5. **Have a hot drink.** An infusion or a spiced hot drink at the end of the day often replaces the urge to snack with a real moment of pause.

## What if I give in?

You're not "giving in": you're eating. Enjoy it, without judging yourself, and move on. One extra square of chocolate doesn't count. What counts is the overall rhythm: regular meals, gentle movement, rest.

## What sugar cravings say about the end of your cycle

Often, a sugar craving is a signal: you're tired, you need comfort, or you haven't eaten enough during the day. Listening to it means asking yourself "what do I really need right now?". Sometimes it's a snack, sometimes a break, sometimes a quiet evening. If your mood is also more fragile on those days, read [why we cry before our period](/en/blog/crying-before-period) too.
