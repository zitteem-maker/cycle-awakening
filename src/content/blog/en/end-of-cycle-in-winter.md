**When the end of your cycle falls in the middle of winter, two kinds of tiredness add up: that of the [luteal phase](/en/blog/luteal-phase), and that of the short days.** Many women feel slower, colder and more sensitive at this time of year. It's not a lack of willpower. It's the moment to adjust your pace to both your cycle and the season.

## Why am I even more tired at the end of my cycle in winter?

At the end of the cycle, energy often drops (see [tiredness before your period](/en/blog/tiredness-before-period)). In winter, natural light decreases, we go out less, move less, and many people feel more tired or gloomy. The two add up: D24 in December doesn't feel the same as D24 in June.

## Should I fight the urge to hibernate?

Not necessarily. Winter invites you to slow down, and so does the end of your cycle. The idea isn't to force yourself, but to keep the few habits that stop you from switching off: light, movement, warming meals, and a little connection.

## How do I get through a winter end of cycle, in practice?

1. **Go and find the light.** Get outside during the day, even for twenty minutes, ideally in the morning or at lunchtime. A walk outside, even on a grey day, is often one of the best pick-me-ups of winter.
2. **Move indoors when it's too cold.** Yoga, Pilates, a gentle session at home: what matters is not stopping altogether (see [which exercise in the luteal phase](/en/blog/exercise-luteal-phase)).
3. **Eat warm and complete.** Thick soups, stews, roasted seasonal vegetables (squash, sweet potato, leek, cabbage), with a source of protein: meals that warm you up and keep you full.
4. **Make your evenings a ritual.** Soft lighting, a hot drink, a blanket, an earlier bedtime: a winter end of cycle deserves slow evenings.
5. **Keep a little connection.** Winter isolates. A call, a coffee, a walk with someone: without forcing yourself out every night, don't cut yourself off from everything.

> Slowing down with the season isn't giving up. It's lasting.

## My winter end-of-cycle ritual

In the evening, around D24: a soup made at the weekend, an infusion, my phone in another room, and an earlier bedtime. In the morning, ten minutes outside before starting the day. Nothing extraordinary, but it's what makes the difference for me.

## When it's no longer just winter

If the tiredness and low mood last all winter, unrelated to your cycle, or if you lose interest in everything, talk to your doctor or a psychologist. It deserves to be taken seriously, and it can be treated.
