**The luteal phase is the second half of your cycle: from ovulation to your period, roughly day 15 to day 28 in a 28-day cycle.** Progesterone dominates, then all hormones fall just before your period. This movement explains the fatigue, the cravings, the bloating and the stronger emotions at the end of the cycle. The good news: once you understand this logic, you can organise your life around it instead of putting up with it.

## What happens in my body during the luteal phase?

Your cycle has four phases: your period, the follicular phase, ovulation, then the luteal phase. After ovulation, what remains of the follicle produces progesterone. This hormone prepares the uterus for a possible pregnancy. If there isn't one, progesterone and oestrogen fall in the last few days, and your period arrives.

So it's not "all in your head". It's a phase of your cycle, with its own rules.

> You are not unstable. You are cyclical.

## Why do I feel so different between day 17 and day 28?

Many women feel, to very different degrees:

- **a slowing down**: less energy, a need for calm, wanting more sleep (see [tiredness before your period](/en/blog/tiredness-before-period));
- **stronger cravings**, often for something sweet (see [sugar cravings before your period](/en/blog/sugar-cravings-before-period));
- **bloating** and slower digestion;
- **stronger emotional sensitivity**, sometimes tears or irritability (see [crying before your period](/en/blog/crying-before-period));
- **lighter sleep** in the last few days.

Every woman experiences her cycle in her own way: these are tendencies, not rules. And if your cycle isn't 28 days long, that's normal. D17–D28 is a marker, not a timetable.

## How do I get through the luteal phase, in practice?

You don't need to change everything. Start with one or two things.

### Nourish without restricting

- **Regular meals**, without skipping any: this avoids big cravings at the end of the day.
- **A complete plate** at every meal: vegetables, a source of protein, good-quality starches, some healthy fats.
- **A planned snack** in the afternoon if you're often hungry around 5 pm, rather than fighting it.
- **Simple, warm dishes** on evenings when energy is low: a soup, a stew made in advance.

No diet, no counting calories: the goal is to support your energy, not to deprive yourself.

### Move the right amount

Adjust the intensity rather than stopping everything. Walking, gentle yoga, Pilates and light strength work often feel good at the end of the cycle. The best marker: how you feel an hour later. Better? You got it right.

### Slow down without guilt

- **Lighten your schedule** around D24–D28 when you can: move what can be moved.
- **Go to bed earlier** and cut down on screens in the evening.
- **Tell the people around you**: "right now, I need more calm" isn't an excuse, it's information.

### Watch your signals

Every evening, write one line about your energy, your mood and your sleep. After two or three cycles, you'll spot your first signs and be able to plan ahead instead of being caught off guard.

## Luteal phase, PMS: what's the difference?

Everyone has a luteal phase. Premenstrual syndrome (PMS) is when end-of-cycle symptoms get in the way of daily life. There is also a severe form, premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD), where mood is very strongly affected: it is treated with a health professional.
